## How coverage works

When a user chooses a payment method, they see the methods available to them, based on their country, and the methods your program has enabled.

You don't need to maintain a list of countries or methods, or branch on them in your code. When ZBD adds a country, users there see the new option without any change on your side.

## Method types

| Type | What it is |
| --- | --- |
| Bank account | Domestic or cross-border transfer to an account the user holds |
| Mobile wallet | Transfer to a wallet, where the market supports it |
| Gift card | Redemption against a catalog, where available |

Some countries support more than one type, and the user chooses.

## What affects timing

Timing depends on the method and the user's country:

- **Near-instant.** Mobile wallets and some domestic schemes settle in minutes.
- **Same or next business day.** Domestic bank transfers in most supported markets.
- **Several business days.** Cross-border bank transfers that go through an intermediary.

Local banking hours and holidays apply to every method, and a payout submitted after the local cut-off starts the next business day.

## Fees

Because the user chooses the method and its speed, the fee for that method comes out of their payout by default. Some methods are free or low cost, and faster ones generally cost more. The widget shows the fee and the final amount before the user confirms.

If a payout involves a currency conversion, the conversion cost is included and shown with the converted amount. If you'd rather cover fees for your users, you can choose to subsidize them.

## Building against this

Method types, [payout statuses](/embedded-payouts/how-payouts-work#payout-statuses) and the shape of the payout instruction are stable. Country availability, limits, fees and the details a user has to enter can change without notice, so read them from ZBD instead of hard-coding them.
