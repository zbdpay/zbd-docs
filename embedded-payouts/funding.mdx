Your payout pool is the balance ZBD holds on your behalf. Every payout draws from it, so it has to be funded before you can pay anyone.
## How funding works
1. You send funds from your treasury to the account ZBD provides for your organization.
2. Funds land in your pool, denominated in your funding currency.
3. Once settled, the full balance is available to pay out.
Funding is always a push from your side, so ZBD never pulls from your account.
## Funding currency and conversion
Your pool holds one funding currency. Recipients are paid in [their own currency](/embedded-payouts/methods-and-coverage), and conversion happens at the point of payout using the rate quoted at that time.
The amount leaving your pool for a given payout can therefore differ from the amount the user receives, and the difference is the conversion.
## Keeping the pool funded

|   |   |
| --- | --- |
| Read your balance | Check available float at any time, including from your own monitoring. |
| Set a threshold | Get notified when the balance falls below a level you set, before it runs dry. |
| Fund ahead of volume | Fund against your expected run rate where volume is predictable, rather than waiting on alerts. |

## When the pool is short
A payout instructed against an underfunded pool fails the [funds check](/embedded-payouts/paying-out), returns a status, and leaves the amount with the user. Nothing is partially paid or silently dropped.
Recipients aren't shown a failure caused by funding, so a short pool doesn't surface as a problem on their side, but it does stop payouts until you top up.
## Settlement timing
Funds are available once they settle with ZBD, and timing depends on how you send them. Plan your top-ups so a settlement window never sits between a user earning and expecting payment.
