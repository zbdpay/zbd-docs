A player can send currency to another player. Nothing is bought and no goods change hands, the value simply moves.
## How a transfer starts
The sending player authorizes it, so a transfer runs on their session and always begins with something they did in your game. Your backend can't move value between two players on its own.
That constraint is deliberate. A transfer gives away something a player owns, so it needs their say-so rather than yours.
## What the receiving user gets
Value that arrives by transfer carries a different source tag than it had in the sender's wallet. What the receiving user can then do with it, including whether they can eventually take it out as money, follows from that tag and the permissions ZBD attaches to it.
[Currencies](/embedded-accounts/currencies) covers source tags and what each one permits.
## Why transfers matter to an economy
An economy where value only ever travels from you to a player and back to you is a closed loop with one participant that matters. Transfers let value circulate between players, which is what makes guilds, favors, trades outside the marketplace and ordinary generosity between friends possible.
It also means your currency starts behaving like something players own, which changes how they treat earning it.
## Limits and checks
Transfers carry limits and checks that ZBD sets and operates. A transfer path is the most attractive route in any economy for someone moving value for reasons that have nothing to do with playing, so it gets more scrutiny than spending does.
These controls evolve. Build against the response you get rather than assuming a fixed set of rules, and surface a refused transfer to the player as a normal outcome instead of an error.
