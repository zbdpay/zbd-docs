Spends happen when a player buys something from you, like a cosmetic, a battle pass, an upgrade or an entry fee.

For player-to-player activity, see [Transfers](/embedded-accounts/transfers) and [Marketplace Sales](/embedded-accounts/marketplace).

## Making a spend

A spend takes the player, the currency and the amount. Your backend makes the call, since your game decides what's being bought and what it costs.

Send an idempotency key so a retry doesn't charge the player twice.

## Where spent currency goes

Spent currency moves to your own account. What you do with it is an economy design decision:

- **Retire it** to take supply out of circulation, which supports the value of what's left
- **Recirculate it** as rewards, which keeps supply flat and costs you nothing new

Most economies do some of both, and the mix is one of your main levers on inflation.

## Spend order

A player's balance is made up of value from different sources, and ZBD draws them down in a fixed order when the player spends. For example, a player with 1,000 units they bought and 500 from rewards who spends 600 is left with a different mix depending on which went first, and that affects what they can cash out. [Currencies](/embedded-accounts/currencies) covers the order.

## Recurring spends

Subscriptions, season passes and other scheduled charges are ordinary spends you make when each period comes due.

Plan for a player's balance falling short between charges. Decide ahead of time whether the subscription lapses, retries or pauses, and let the player know.

## When a spend fails

A spend fails if the balance can't cover it, and nothing is taken.

Where you can, check the player's spendable balance before showing a price. It's a better experience to show a shortfall early than to refuse a purchase after the player confirms.
