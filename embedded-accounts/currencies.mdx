An economy runs on one or more currencies, and there are two kinds you can use.
**Fiat** is real-world money such as dollars or euros. Its value is its face value and there's nothing for you to define.
**Virtual currency** is one you define yourself. You decide what it's called, what it's worth in each direction, and whether it converts back to money at all.
Some publishers run an entire economy on fiat, with players holding and moving real money. Others define their own currency, and a lot of economies use both together.
## Convertible or not
The first decision on a virtual currency is whether it converts back to real money.
**Convertible** currencies carry a redemption rate and can leave as real money. This is what makes rewards, marketplace proceeds and cash out possible, and it's also where the compliance rules land: what can leave, when, and for whom.
**Non-convertible** currencies are spend-only. No redemption rate, no cash value, no exit. A grind currency that buys cosmetics doesn't need one, and making it convertible brings obligations a pure game mechanic never has to carry.
Most economies want both: something players earn and spend freely, and something that carries real value.
How a player came by value decides what they can do with it, so value you granted behaves differently from value they bought, which behaves differently again from value another player sent them. ZBD applies that classification when the value lands, based on how it arrived. [Cash outs](/embedded-accounts/cash-out) covers which value can leave and when.
## Naming and scope
A currency carries a currency code and a display name. The code identifies it in the API and doesn't change. The display name is what players see in your game.
Keeping those separate is what makes the next part work, because you also choose how a currency maps to your projects.
Link one currency to several projects and it behaves identically across all of them, so a player earning Gems in one of your games holds the same thing in another under the same rules.
The alternative is a variant per project, each with its own conversion rates. Give every variant the display name Gem and players see one currency across your whole catalog while each game runs its own economy underneath.

<Note>
  **ISO currency codes are reserved** and cannot be used for virtual currencies. Your display name should distinctly represent your virtual currency and should not overlap with real-world currency.
</Note>

## Units and denomination
Currencies differ in their lowest denomination, which is the smallest amount that can exist.
USD's lowest denomination is a cent, so a dollar is 100 of them. Bitcoin's is a satoshi, so one bitcoin is 100,000,000. Balances are always held as whole numbers of the lowest denomination, which is why ZBD doesn't impose decimal places or add zeros behind the scenes.
For a currency you define, the lowest denomination is your choice, and it's worth thinking past the price of your cheapest item.
Say your currency is Gems. If the lowest denomination is one Gem, you can't represent half a Gem, which holds up right until you take a 10% cut on a 5-Gem marketplace sale and have nowhere to put the 0.5.
Make the lowest denomination a hundredth of a Gem and the same economy works:

|   | Units | Shown to the player |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Cosmetic | 100 | 1 Gem |
| Marketplace sale | 500 | 5 Gems |
| Your 10% cut | 50 | 0.5 Gems |
| Seller receives | 450 | 4.5 Gems |

Players still see Gems. What you display stays yours, and the denomination only sets how finely the economy can divide underneath.
Pick one small enough that your smallest price and your smallest fee both land on a whole number.
## Virtual currency conversion rates
You set two conversion rates independently, one for each direction.

|   | Controls |
| --- | --- |
| Purchase rate | The amount of virtual currency a player gets for their money going in |
| Redemption rate | What a unit of virtual currency is worth when it's converted to fiat at cashout |

The two don't have to mirror each other. The gap between them is the spread your economy runs on, and players work it out fast, so it's worth setting deliberately.
Rates apply at the moment of conversion, so a player converting today gets today's rate even if they earned the currency months ago.
## What to lock before launch

| Decision | Why it's painful later |
| --- | --- |
| Convertible or not | Units already in circulation would gain cash value they were never issued with |
| Currency code | The code is fixed once set |
| Lowest denomination | Changing it means migrating every balance and entry |
| Conversion rates | The redemption rate decides what a unit is worth when a player converts it out |
| Scope | Splitting a shared currency into per-project variants after launch means migrating balances between them |
