Each account also records every transaction and carries the permissions that decide what the player can do.

## The objects

| Object | What it is |
| --- | --- |
| User | The player. You create one using your own identifier for them. |
| Account | Their financial record with ZBD. One per user. |
| Wallet | Where a balance in a given currency lives. Wallets are currency-specific. |

Creating a user sets up the account and a wallet for each currency in your project, so you can credit them straight away.

## Creating an account

You create accounts from your backend with your own identifier for the player, a country code, and confirmation that they accepted your terms. No identity documents or personal data are needed.

Players can hold a balance, earn and spend without verifying. Verification is required when a player:

- Loads a fiat wallet
- Sells an item for fiat
- Cashes out

## How balances are calculated

A balance is the sum of every ledger entry for that wallet. Each entry has a source tag recording where the value came from.

The total is what a player can spend. What they can cash out depends on the source tags behind it, so a player with a balance of 1,700 might be able to cash out all, some or none of it. ZBD sets the rules for each tag, and [Currencies](/embedded-accounts/currencies) explains how they work.

## Multiple wallets

Each currency gets its own wallet automatically. You can also create extra wallets in the same currency to keep value separate, for example a seasonal event balance.

## Your own wallets

Your organization holds a wallet per currency, and you fund a project wallet per currency for each game. Keeping only a game's expected payouts in its project wallet limits how much a leaked key or fraud can reach.

## Reading balances

You can read a wallet's balance, the entries behind it, and how much is available to cash out. We recommend reading these from ZBD instead of tracking balances yourself, since source tags and conversions affect what a player can do with their balance.
