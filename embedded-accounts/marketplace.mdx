## Who does what

You run the marketplace: listings, search, matching, pricing and disputes. ZBD doesn't see any of it.

ZBD handles settlement. You send an instruction with the two players, the amount, the currency and an item reference, and ZBD moves the money.

The marketplace can live inside your game, on a companion site, or on a standalone platform such as a mod portal. Whoever runs it is the publisher.

## Settling a sale

A sale usually pays more than one party: the seller gets their proceeds, you get your cut, and ZBD takes its fee. These all settle together, so either every part lands or none of it does.

## Seller verification

A seller has to be verified before a sale that settles in fiat. Sales in your own virtual currency don't need it, since no real money leaves.

Where it applies, this is the most important part of a marketplace integration to get right. A buyer can purchase at any time, often long after the listing went up and while the seller is offline. If the seller isn't verified, the sale fails and nobody is there to fix it.

We recommend prompting sellers to verify when they create their first listing. It's worth doing even where a sale doesn't require it. Any seller who wants to cash out will need to verify eventually, and it's easier for them before they're waiting to get paid.

## What the seller receives

Sale proceeds land in the seller's balance and can be cashed out straight away. That gives players a quick route to real money, which can change how they use the rest of your economy.

## What to send with a settlement

Along with the two players and the amount, send:

- The listing reference
- When the listing was created
- Whether it was an open listing anyone could buy, or a direct deal between two named players

The last two help ZBD's risk checks. A listing that was open for a day before it sold looks very different from one created and bought in the same second, and ZBD can't tell them apart without this information.
